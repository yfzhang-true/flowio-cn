Introduction Manufacturing industries are preferred automation and using robots to perform necessary assembly operations given by an assembly planner. Assembly sequence plan (ASP) generation is rec- ognized as a crucial activity in the production phase because it can inﬂuence entire assembly layout and provide information about the essential jigs, ﬁxtures to support robotic actions [1,2]. A well- organized viable ASP reduces overall cost, time by optimizing the tool travels and total number of changes in assembly directions [3–5]. An assembly planner should be capable of performing neces- sary predicate testing and optimality criteria to generate such a viable ASP [6]. Generating an optimal assembly sequence plan is considered as a NP-hard problem since a product containing ‘n’ number of com- ponents will have a solution space of n! (linear assembly sequences alone) whereas less than 1% of the solution space is valid for optimality assessment [7–9]. Numerous exact methods, heuristics and Artiﬁcal Intelligence (AI) methods can produce near optimal solution by considering different criteria such as time, cost, tool changes, directional changes after necessary predicate assessment [10–23]. It can be observed from Table 1 that the existed approaches considered the objective function to minimize the number of directional changes, tool changes and tool travel dis- tance. However a solution is said to be feasible based on its ability to qualify necessary assembly predicates. Primary assembly pred- icates such as liaison, geometrical feasibility are considered in most of the ASP generation methods whereas only few studies given importance for stability and mechanical feasibility predicate. Graph and matrix based representation methods are followed by the researchers to represent the essential assembly information. Bahubalendruni et al. presented an extensive study on the various graphs such as non-directional blocking graphs, interference graphs, AND/OR graphs, petri-net graphs etc., and opinioned that matrix based representation is most suitable for automated solu- tion generation [5]. It is observed that the graph based methods required human intervention for the testing of advances predicate to reduce the practical complexities in the automation of method. The primary assembly predicate such as GF testing is subjected to conceptual limitation which can identify collision free paths in principal axes directions ð/C6 X; /C6 Y; /C6 ZÞ. Most of the instances, human intervention is needed to ﬁnd feasible paths in oblique ori- ented assemblies [37,38]. To reduce the complexity and increase the consis- tency, Pan tried to automate the process of extracting Interference matrix (IM) from standard for exchange of product data (STEP) ﬁles [3].IM is an N /C2 N binary matrix (N represents the number of parts in the product) in which the individual elements ‘‘0” and ‘‘1” rep- resent the feasibility and in-feasibility to perform assembly opera- tions. Wolter proposed a method to represent the GF information as precedence relations but it requires human intervention to answer the queries related to other assembly predicates [40]. Later Xu extracted IMs from 3D Computer Aided Design (CAD) models by moving each part in the assembly in the presence of other parts and evaluating collision at each step in Simens NX [41]. Development of ODIM The ODIM is a n /C2 n decimal matrix which consist of necessary information about the interference-free feasible directions. Here ‘n’ represents the total number of parts existed in a product and the developed ODIM is aimed to represent all feasible directions at machine readable format. The procedure for automatic extraction of ODIM from a CADE is presented. 2.1. Extraction of Liaison Matrix from the assembly Liaison/contact matrix is a n /C2 n binary matrix which consists of the elements 1, 0 to provide information about the existence of contact/no contact between two parts. It can be observed from the Fig. 3 that the parts 1 and 2 are in contact with each other. Here the liaison between parts 1 and 2 is represented by L (1, 2) =1 and L (2, 1) = 1. The Liaison Matrix for the given 3D CAD model is created by using clash analysis in 4 th generation CAD Tool. The module can be used to analyze three types of conﬂicts namely negative conﬂict (Interference), null (contact) and positive conﬂict (clearance)) as shown in Fig. 4 . The Liaison matrix is used to minimize the total number of directions required for generating ODIM. 2.2. Extraction of feasible vectors For successful assembly operations, the parts should be moved in collision-free directions. A part can possess inﬁnite number of directions as shown in Fig. 5a, however only a few of them are said to be feasible for performing assembly operations. A normal pro- jected from the surface of a part is taken for interference test and Fig. 5b represents the 8 normals projected from 8 different surfaces of a part. The extracted directions were represented in vector form, for example, the vector ½100/C138 represents þXdirection. The direc- tions obtained from each part in the assembly are ﬁltered out to remove any duplicate directions. The unique directions obtained from each part after the ﬁltering process were stored in a multi- dimensional array list called All Directions (AD). 2.3. Non-Conditional Interference Checking(NCIC) In this process, the redundant directions are removed after liai- son based interference checking. The every existing part in the pro- duct is tested for interference in the presence of other parts. For real-time interference checking, the parts have to be moved from one location in 3D space to another along a speciﬁed direction. The distance required for moving the parts is calculated using Bounding boxes. As Axis Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) is computa- tionally less expensive they were implemented in this research. An AABB is simply a parallelopiped whose axes are parallel to the principal axes (X, Y, Z) of the coordinate system in which they were deﬁned. Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) is similar to AABB but the axes of OBB are need not be parallel with principal axes of the coor- dinate system. Fig. 6a illustrates the AABB for 8-part holder assem- bly which is parallel to principal axes and Fig. 6 b for OBB in other possible direction. As the interference checking has to be carried out in various directions a reference axis system (U, V, W) is cre- ated for every direction and an AABB is generated based on the ref- erence axis system. If GCS is considered as the reference coordinate system, the bounding boxes can be created at such place and referred as OBB. The OBBs are created by generating extremums in a given direction. The major limitation in CAD systems has been occured at this instant in which the extremums can be extracted from the LCS of individual parts. All the direction vectors are extracted in GCS by creating the extremums of individual parts to avoid arise of errors with their extraction in LCS. ODIM integration with Assembly planner An existed assembly planner named Part concatenation method (PCM) proposed by Bahubalendruni et al. has been adopted to inte- grate the proposed ODIM [42]. PCM is an automated method which can produce an optimal and practically feasible solution to perform assembly actions in the principal axes at minimal computational effort by considering advanced predicates. The necessary informa- tion such as liaison matrix, stability matrix given along with ODIM to perform ASP through oblique orientations. The extraction of sta- bility matrix is presented below. 3.1. Stability matrix The stability matrix ensures the generated part combinations (subassembly) are not stable, partially stable or permanent stable. A subassembly is said to be permanently stable (dynamically stable) if there is no relative motion between individual parts due to orientation or natural forces like gravity. If any existed part in the subassembly is failed to maintain the contact with mating parts due to orientation, then the subassembly is said to be par- tially stable (statically stable). Various conditions of stability are illustrated in Fig. 15 . The stability matrix is an N /C2 N decimal matrix which consists of elements 0, 1 and 2 to no stability, partial stability and permanent stability respectively [42]. 3.2. Results and discussions A comparative assessment for the proposed NASPM with the existed ASP methods is not possible with the products made up of parts located in oblique orientations. It can be observed from the Table 6 that the versatile PCM, TLBO is unable to generate solu- tion for the products represented in Fig. 20 and Fig. 21 which require human intervention. Hence the 16-part transmission assembly is taken into consideration to compare the performance in terms of computational time required for solution generation. The optimality in solution is explained through number of levels offered to complete the overall assembling process. It can be pro- ven that the proposed method consume less time, minimum num- ber of directions over other ASP methods and the generated solution require 7 levels compared to 15 levels from PCM, TLBO. Thus these results are strongly indicating the robustness of the proposed ODIM and effective integration with an assembly planner to solve the combinatorial problem. 6. Conclusion In this research, a novel automated method named as ODIM has been proposed and developed to perform GF testing in oblique ori- entations. It is found that the technical limitations in CAD system made automated GF testing is conﬁned to principal axes and demanded human intervention for parts located in oblique orienta- tions. The bounding boxes such as AABB and OBB is utilized to test the parts existed in principal axes and oblique orientations respec- tively. The information about geometric feasible assembly direc- tions are represented in vector format to support encoding at further stages in solution generation. The developed module is effectively integrated with existing ASP method and its working is tested by applying different real time products. The results are proven that NASPM can generate optimal solution in the auto- mated manner for the parts located in oblique orientations and principal axes. The following novelties have been contributed to the ASP research: /C15 Liaison based checking has been performed to reduce the com- putational effort in GF testing by identifying the most probable direction and data redundancy elimination by ﬁltering the sim- ilar feasible directions. /C15 Conditional and Non-conditional interference checking con- cepts are proposed to identify GF at different assembling instances. /C15 Vector based information representations is proposed for encoding the directions in the binary format to support solution generation in automated manner. /C15 The optimality in solution is proven through the minimum number of levels offered to complete overall assembly process. The future scope of this research is to extend the proposed ODIM concept to solve the disassembly sequence planning prob- lem in repair and maintenance perspective.
