BiFlu2: A Miniaturized Electro-Fluidic Circuit for All-On-Board
Control of Hydraulically-Actuated Biorobotic Organs
Amin Hamed Lalaklo, Federica Semproni, Student Member, IEEE, Veronica Iacovacci, Member, IEEE,
and Arianna Menciassi, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract— This paper presents the design and implementa-
tion of BiFlu2, a compact electro-fluidic circuit engineered for
implantable biorobotic organs with hydraulic actuators. BiFlu2
enables fluidic control with a limited number of components,
i.e. two bistable 3-way valves for bidirectional fluid transfer
and an additional one for fluid (de)multiplexing. The system is
designed to operate with minimal power consumption, making
it suitable for long-term operation upon implantation. The
design incorporates an ESP32 microprocessor for wireless
remote control, as well as pressure sensors to monitor real-time
actuators’ performance. BiFlu2 has been tested and validated
through simulations and experimental setups, demonstrating
efficient hydraulic control of an artificial detrusor integrated
with a soft artificial bladder. The experimental results show
reliable performance, low power consumption, and effective
actuator control for the voiding process of an artificial bladder
provided with a hydraulic detrusor. This study highlights the
BiFlu2 potential for use in fluidically actuated implantable
biorobotic organs, where compact size and energy efficiency
are critical. Refer to the supplementary video for a summary
of our findings.
I. INTRODUCTION
Over the past decade, soft robotics has witnessed signifi-
cant advancement, with different innovative designs emerg-
ing to address various applications [1].
In this context, among the possible actuation methods,
fluidic actuation has become the most widely used in soft
devices, due to safe interactions with the surroundings,
simpler fabrication, ability to generate substantial forces
and strokes, etc [2], [3]. This method employs pressurized
fluids—either gases or liquids—to inflate specific sections
of the actuator, resulting in bending or other forms of
deformation, depending on the system’s requirements and
design [4]. From a control perspective, these actuators often
require dedicated electro-fluidic hardware, including multiple
pumps, valves, and fluid sources to effectively distribute fluid
among multiple actuators to perform the desired tasks [5]–
[8]. While these setups enable precise control over the move-
ment and deformation of soft structures, they also introduce
significant challenges concerning system size, weight, and
energy consumption [9]. These factors can limit the overall
efficiency and practicality of the overall systems, particularly
in applications where compactness and lightweight designs
are essential.
When considering the placement of soft robots based
on fluidic actuators, inside the human body, addressing the

Several advanced fluidic IBROs have been reported in the

artificial detrusor (AD) for urinary bladder controlled void-

exploited bellows actuators devised to constitute hydraulic
AD to control voiding in an artificial bladder (AB) while
reproducing detrusor functions [13], [14].
Most of the focus in the aforementioned works has been
on the design of actuators rather than on the electro-fluidic
circuits necessary for controlling those actuators. As a result,
their control systems tend to be bulky, unoptimized, and
lack complete all-on-board integration, making them more
suitable for bench testing than for real-world and implantable
applications.

In this study, to address the shortcomings regarding the
control of fluidic IBROs, a miniaturized electro-fluidic circuit
hereby defined as BiFlu2, is proposed (Figure 1). Leveraging
small bistable valves and optimally designed fluidic path-
ways, bidirectional fluidic control of more than one actuator
at low power is proposed. BiFlu2 is compatible with both
pneumatically and hydraulically actuated systems, as the
pump can be easily adjusted to accommodate the specific
type of operation, whether gas or liquid. In this article,
the circuit validation was performed with a hydraulic AD
mounted on an AB prototype [13].

Section II presents the specifications, design, and fabrication
of BiFlu2. Section III is dedicated to testing the BiFlu2
with an implantable AB, including hydraulic-driven actuators
for the voiding process. Finally, Section IV is allocated for
conclusion and future works.
II. MATERIALS AND METHODS
A. Fluidic pathways design
One of the most important aspects of fluidic control
systems is transferring the fluid both inside and outside
the actuators in a bidirectional manner. Most pumps on
the market are unidirectional, providing limited options for
bidirectional models. Furthermore, the available bidirectional
pumps are usually bulky (e.g., with dimensions exceeding 80
mm × 50 mm × 50 mm), and while there are some very
small options, they generally offer extremely low flow rates,
typically in the µl/min range [16], [17]. Thus, a simple valve-
based design is proposed to convert unidirectional pumps

into bidirectional ones by utilizing two three-way valves. The
schematic illustrating the operational principles of the design
is shown in Figure 2(a). When valve 1 (V1) is in S2 mode
and valve 2 (V2) is in S1 mode, the pump transfers fluid
from the reservoir to the actuator (blue lines). Conversely,
when V1 is in S1 mode and V2 in S2 mode, fluid flows
from the actuator to the reservoir (orange dashed lines).
Considering the selected valves and pump, a 3-way valve
has been utilized as a (de)multiplexer. Consequently, the final
system design incorporates three 3-way valves, enhancing
the overall functionality and flexibility of the fluidic control
mechanism. The design of the fluidic pathways (Figure 2(b))
takes into account several key factors, including the selection
of the most direct routes, maximizing path diameter to
enhance flow efficiency, and minimizing the overall system
size. To achieve this, the valves have been placed as close
to each other as possible. Subsequently, the diameter of the
paths has been increased to the extent that no interference
occurs between them. This led to a path diameter of 2.2 mm
and a minimum distance between paths of 0.6 mm. Reducing
the distance below 0.6 mm leads to path wall rupture and
interference between the paths. In this configuration, quarter-
bends—defined as bends in fluidic channels that change the
flow direction by 90 degrees—have been integrated into
the design. These bends help reduce abrupt changes that
create turbulence and energy loss, thereby improving flow
efficiency. By extending the fluidic pathways and adding 2-
way or 3-way valves, the number of outputs can be increased.
In general, n 3-way valves in the multiplexer section allow
for n+1 outputs. This framework is adaptable to different
sizes, depending on valve type and required dimensions.
Moreover, the design includes additional slots to accom-
modate the other circuit components, such as the battery and
pump, ensuring that all necessary elements are integrated
seamlessly. This comprehensive approach not only stream-
lines the assembly process but also maximizes the available
space within the system. Finally, the completed design was
printed using VisiJet M3 Crystal, as shown in Figure 3.
B. Hardware overview
At the heart of BiFlu2 is a single board computer (ESP32-
C3 Mini Development Board, Waveshare Electronics), cho-

sen for its low power consumption (Idle mode: 0.03-0.1
Watts), high performance, and compact size (23.5 mm × 18
mm). It comprises two main functional blocks: the control
block and the communication block. The control block
executes data processing and manages the fluidic system
operations. Meanwhile, the communication block utilizes the
integrated Wi-Fi and Bluetooth modules for remote control
and real-time monitoring.
A critical component of the BiFlu2 system is the valve, as
monostable valves typically require a continuous current to
maintain their state, resulting in high energy consumption. To
significantly reduce power consumption, a bistable valve was
selected. Unlike monostable valves, bistable valves maintain
their state (open or closed) without the need for continuous
electrical power. This feature makes them highly energy-
efficient. As a result, three 5 V bistable 3-way valve (LHL
Series Latching 3-Way Control Solenoid Valve, The Lee
Company) were chosen, each requiring only 5.5 mJ to switch
their state. This low-energy consumption is particularly ad-
vantageous in battery-powered systems. The valve features
an inlet and outlet diameter of approximately 2 mm and
supports a liquid flow rate of up to 250 ml/min.
To control the valve states and manage the pump, two
Dual Motor Driver Carriers (DRV8833, Texas Instruments)
were employed. These drivers facilitate bidirectional fluid
flow control and allow for precise pump activation. A 3.7 V
to 5 V voltage booster has been integrated into the design
to ensure efficient power conversion for a lithium battery
with a capacity of 850 mAh. In addition to the valves, three
gauge pressure sensors (ABPMRRV600MG2A3, Honeywell)
with the I2C protocol have been incorporated to monitor the
pressure levels within the pump and actuators. These sensors
provide real-time data on actuator performance. Since all
three sensors share the same I2C address, an I2C multiplexer
(TCA9548A, Texas Instruments) was introduced to enable
simultaneous reading from multiple sensors, ensuring effi-
cient communication and data processing within the system.
Finally, a compact yet powerful 5 V unidirectional water
pump (SP 270 EC-L, Schwarzer Precision) was selected for
the system. Despite its small size, the pump can provide high
relative suction pressure (up to -300 mbar) and discharge
relative pressure (up to 1000 mbar).
C. Simulation
In selecting an appropriate pump for the fluidic system,
it is critical to consider the influence of pressure drop on
the overall performance. Pressure drop arises from frictional
losses within the fluidic pathways and changes in flow
direction and velocity. To ensure the selected pump operates
effectively within the system, a comprehensive simulation
was conducted using SolidWorks software to analyze these
pressure losses across various operating conditions. Addi-
tionally, the simulation aimed to visualize fluid movement
through the system under different scenarios, including flow
from the reservoir to the actuators and vice versa.
By evaluating the pressure drop, the simulation provides
valuable insights into the optimality of the selected pump
configuration. This thorough assessment not only aids in
selecting a pump that meets the required flow rates but also
verifies its efficient operation within the fluidic system.
For simplicity, the pump and valves were replaced with
straight tubing to isolate the effects of the flow path itself.
The simulation assumed atmospheric pressure at the outlets,
while the inlet was assigned with specified flow rates. Given
that the pump maximum flow rate is 100 ml/min, the
simulation was conducted for a range of flow rates between
0 and 100 ml/min. Simultaneously, the pressure drop across
the three identical valves was calculated using data from the
manufacturer’s datasheet [18].
D. Experimental test
To characterize the BiFlu2, experiments were conducted
in which the inlet and outlet were connected to beakers
maintained at room temperature. This setup allowed for a
controlled environment to evaluate the performance of the
system. Tests were performed for durations ranging between
1 and 5 minutes, with three repetitions per interval to ensure
reliable results. During each test, the procedure involved first
transferring water from the inlet to the outlet, followed by
recording the data. Then, for the same duration, the process
was reversed. Key performance metrics, including BiFlu2
flow rate, pump current, water volume, differential pressure,
and pump temperature were recorded throughout the experi-
ment. In addition to these parameters, the temperature of the
pump was continuously monitored to assess any variations
that might occur during the operation.
E. V alidation
Following the characterization phase, the system was
validated to ensure it met predefined criteria, including
operational efficiency, response accuracy, stability under
experimental testing conditions. The experiment involved
activating the hydraulic actuators through BiFlu2 to achieve
a target water volume of 25 ml within each balloon. This
specific volume is essential for the effective operation of
the actuators, enabling them to exert force on the AB and
facilitate the voiding process [13]. Initially, the actuator

III. RESULTS
A. Simulation
Simulations revealed that the maximum pressure drop,
resulting from the pump maximum flow rate of 100 ml/min,
was 20 mbar (2000 Pa).
The results are shown in Figure 5, when the flow rate
is below 30 ml/min, the pressure drops across the system
exhibit minimal variation. Specifically, at a flow rate of 30
ml/min, the pressure drop values for the valves and the path
are measured at 21.75 mbar and 5.21 mbar, respectively,
resulting in a difference of 16.54 mbar. However, as the flow
rate exceeds 30 ml/min, this difference becomes significantly
more pronounced. For instance, at a flow rate of 80 ml/min,
the pressure drop values rise to 317.4 mbar and 24.2 mbar,
respectively, leading to a difference of 293.2 mbar. It clearly
demonstrates that the pressure drop caused by the fluidic
pathways is negligible compared to the drop across the
valves. This indicates that the system is well-designed and
that the fluidic path has an insignificant impact on the overall
pressure drop.

at approximately 140 mA, accompanied by a consistent

demonstrated a linear relationship across the various time
intervals tested. Additionally, the stable pressure differential

To further evaluate the system’s operational efficiency,
power consumption was measured. When the pump and
valves were turned off, the integrated circuits and sensors
consumed a current of 43 mA. During operation, each valve
required 5.5 mJ for a brief period of 10 ms, while the pump
itself consumed approximately 140 mA. If we disregard the
10 ms power demand from the valves, the maximum power
consumption of the system was calculated to be 0.66 Watts.
However, when the valves operated simultaneously with
other components, the total power consumption increased to
1.21 Watts during the 10 ms operation period. With the 3.7
V , 850 mAh battery, the system can operate continuously for
approximately 250 minutes, taking into account a reasonable
number of valve on/off cycles. These data indicate that
the system exhibits very low power consumption overall,
highlighting its efficiency during operation.
These results demonstrate that BiFlu2 maintains a low
power footprint, making it suitable for applications where
energy efficiency is crucial. Its sustainable performance,
along with a compact size and minimal temperature variation,
further reinforces the viability of BiFlu2 as an efficient
solution for fluidic control in fluidic IBROs.
C. V alidation
According to the estimated system flow rate, approxi-
mately 25 ml of liquid could be filled within an estimated
duration of 22 s. To assess the reliability of this estimation,
the experiment was conducted in triplicate. For each trial,
the actuator was first filled with water, then fully drained,
and the expelled water was weighed. Results demonstrated a
consistent volume of 25 ± 0.5 ml in each instance. Figure 8
depicts the actuator’s internal pressure profiles across trials,
indicating a peak pressure of 74 ± 1.41 mbar when the
actuator is not in contact with the AB. However, when the
actuator and the AB are in direct contact (Figure 4), the
filling time increases to 48 s, with the water volume within
each of the actuators under these conditions measured at 25.5
±1 ml. The reason for this increase in time is a reduction in
the system flow rate due to the increased pressure exerted
on the actuator by the force of the filled bladder. This
results in the internal pressure within the actuators reaching a
maximum value of 110 ± 7 mbar when fully filled (Figure 8).
To assess the overall efficiency and accuracy of the system
under this situation, the voiding process was repeated three
times. These tests demonstrated that the complete voiding of

IV. CONCLUSIONS
In this work, we have presented BiFlu2, a novel and highly
compact electro-fluidic circuit for all-on-board controlling
fluidic IBROs. The system architecture with bistable 3-
way valves minimizes power consumption, making it viable
for long-term use in medical applications. Additionally, the
system can be adapted for pneumatic applications since the
valves are compatible with both liquids and gases, requiring
only a pump modification. Experimental testing on an arti-
ficial bladder, using balloon hydraulic actuators to control
voiding, demonstrates BiFlu2’s effectiveness in achieving
reliable, bidirectional fluid control with minimal thermal
impact. Future work will focus on analyzing the pressure
data obtained from the study to enhance the method of
controlling the system. This analysis aims to identify patterns
and correlations that may inform more effective control
strategies, ultimately leading to improved system respon-
siveness and stability. Additionally, efforts will be directed
toward optimizing the design for implantation, potentially
by encapsulating the device in silicone or other suitable
materials, while also exploring its applications in other
fluidic-driven implantable biorobotic organs. For instance,
the system’s ability to control two actuators at the outlet
not only facilitates the regulation of the AD but also allows
for the simultaneous management of the bladder outlet using
an artificial sphincter, thereby enabling a fully automated
voiding process.
