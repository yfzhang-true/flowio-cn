Sports has emerged as a promising learning space for computing concepts and methods for socially and disciplinarilyminoritized communities, in particular youth of color. Bringing a constructionist way of learning computing in these sportscontexts – by making authentic publicly shared and used artifacts by youth, presents a powerful potential for combattingminoritization of learners’ experiences, interests, and ways of thinking. Although there are wearable tool kits for learningpurposes, they are not expressly designed and developed for sports and athleticslearningenvironments.Currenttoolsetsfor making wearables make accessible aspects of making wearables, but there are limitations in terms of multimodalsensors, customization, and material rigidity that are key for athletic spaces given the dynamics of doingphysical activity.Intersectionality we see efforts fromthe soft robotics community to make concepts, methods, and tools fromsoftroboticswidely accessible to diverse learners. This set of challenges and opportunities has the potential to provide bi-directoonalopportunities for youth learners as well as designers of soft robotics tools. For the former, soft robotics can be anempowering tool to learners’ broader imagination and implementation potentials for building new designs relevant topersonal needs in sports spaces. For the latter, it is an opportunity to democratize soft robotics and extend its use bydeveloping newtools and processessuitableforthesportslearningcontext.Inthiswork,wepresent1)preliminaryfindingsfrom a sketching-based making activity fromyouth imagining productive uses of technology in sports that we believe aresuitable cases for soft robotics, 2) learning initiatives involvingyouthofcolor,and3)designconsiderationsforsoftroboticstools designers. Altogether, this work contributes design strategies for designing soft robotics-integrated learningexperiences for youth of color as well as design requirements for soft robotics tools.
