2.2 1DSensingSystem
Texoskeleton’s sensing system comprised of 1D triboelectric
nanogenerator (TENG) sensors to assess the movements. The
triboelectricsensorsweredesignedandevaluatedforwristmove-

Herein,TENGsensorsweredesignedwithaflexible,stretchable
architecture to facilitate comfort, accuracy, and unobtrusive
sensing,andtheself-poweredoperationofthesesensorshelpsto
simplify the system complexity and operation. Furthermore, to
simplify the manufacturability and reduce the costs, the TENG
designutilizedanidenticalsiliconetubedesignasthe1Dactuator
as its external triboelectric element, which was then integrated
withamodifiedtextileyarnplacedatitscore,whichactedasthe
inner triboelectric element. Having identical outer surfaces and
dimensionsofthe1Dsensorsand1Dactuatorsarealsointended
tosupportthedesignandintegrationprocessoftheTexoskeleton.
The fabrication process of the 1D triboelectric sensor using
the aforementioned core-sheath yarn structure is depicted in
Figure4a.Thecorecomponentwasamodifiedtextileyarndesign,
consisting of a silver-plated high-conductive polyamide (nylon
6,6) yarn (Shieldex 235/36 2-ply HC) with a resistivity of 80
Ω/m,whichactedastheelectrodeofthe1Dtriboelectricsensor.
Thiswascoateduniformlyandconformallywithathermoplastic
polyurethane (TPU) layer, creating the positive triboelectric
surface. The outer sheath of the 1D triboelectric sensor, which
acted as the negative triboelectric surface, was fabricated using
Ecoflex 0030, in the form of a hollow cylindrical sleeve, using
the same method that was used to create the 1D actuator
yarns in Section2.1.1. The core yarn was then inserted into the
cylindricaloutersleeve,creatingthecore-sheath1Dtriboelectric
sensor.
Within this sensor design, the difference in diameter between
the core yarn and the outer sleeve created a gap between the
innersurfaceoftheoutersheath(Ecoflex),andtheoutersurface
of the inner core (TPU) (FigureS7a). When the 1D triboelectric
sensor is subjected to deformations such as stretching/releasing
orbending,theoutercoreandtheinnersheathsurfacescontact
each other, triboelectrically charging these surfaces. Therefore,
theTPUlayergetspositivelychargedandtheEcoflexsurfacegets
negatively charged (FigureS7b). Furthermore, the electrode is
connected to a ground plane (a conductive patch), through an
electrical measurement unit. During its use, the deformation of
this yarn changes the gap between these two charged surfaces
(FigureS7b–e).Theresultantchangeintheelectricfieldsinduces
a potential difference between the electrode and the ground,
resultingintheoutputgenerationofthe1Dtriboelectricsensor.

subjected to repeated compression-decompression cycles (up to

able to withstand over 3000 compression-decompression cycles

3 Discussion
Wepresentanovelapproachfordesigningwearableexoskeletons
by embedding 1D cylindrical-shaped devices directly within
textiles, allowing actuators and sensors to be positioned across
variousregionsofthebody.Thismethodofconstructingexoskele-
tons ensures that the textiles conformability is not significantly
impacted by the added electronics. Moreover, the Texoskele-
ton design with dedicated channels for 1D devices supports
easy replacement of malfunctioning components and simplifies
the recycling process. This paper introduces the first textile
exoskeleton sleeve prototype constructed using 1D actuators
and sensors. The 1D triboelectric sensors integrated into the
Texoskeleton sleeve can accurately classify wrist movements,
including extension-flexion, ulnar-radial deviation, supination,
and pronation. Meanwhile, the embedded 1D actuators can
providecontrolledforcesatspecificregionsofthewrist.
Results indicate that the 1D actuator performance is highly
dependentonbothmaterialcompositionandtextilearchitecture.
Single-material actuators showed superior lifting capabilities
when embedded in cut-and-sew fabrics. In contrast, the two-
materialactuatorsprovideintrinsicbendingbehaviorthatisinde-
pendent of the surrounding textile and demonstrate enhanced
liftingperformancewhenembeddedwithinknittedfabrics.This
inherent capability affords greater design flexibility and enables
theuseofawidervarietyoftextilematerialsinthedevelopmentof
Texoskeletonsystems.Overall,thesefindingsconfirmthatalign-
ing1Dactuatoryarndesignwiththeoutertextilearchitectureis
essentialforachievingoptimalfunctionalperformance.

everydayclothingtomonitormovementsofindividualsandoffer
personalizedresistance/assistanceforhome-basedrehabilitation

achieve this aim, leading toward intelligent textile exoskeletons
(Texoskeletons) that are soft, lightweight, and capable of per-

of industries, and their role in personalized rehabilitation is
especially promising. They offer a practical option for people
who have limited access to professional rehabilitative care. To
furtherenhancetheTexoskeletonforrehabilitation,technologies
such as 1D accelerometer yarns and 1D temperature sensing
yarns demonstrated in our previous work [53, 54], can also be

4.5 CustomInteractiveFilteringToolandthe
MachineLearningAlgorithmforthe1D
TriboelectricSensor
The custom interactive filtering tool provided a graphical inter-
facewithadjustablethreshold(h)andtemporalrange(d),where
the user could iteratively tune the parameters to accurately
detectpulseboundaries(Figure 4d).Oncethedesiredparameters
were set, the tool automatically identified rising and falling
edges in the filtered signal to mark pulse start and end points.
These cutoff points were subsequently used to construct a
labeled pulse database. Each pulse segment was extracted from
its original time series and associated with its corresponding
gestureclass(extension-flexion,radialdeviation-ulnardeviation,
pronation, and supination). To ensure uniform input size, all
pulses were resampled to a fixed length of 200 samples and
normalized. This curated dataset was then split into training
and testing subsets with an 80/20 ratio while preserving class
distribution.
The model architecture of the 1D CNN (Figure4e), consisted of
four sequential convolutional blocks (Conv1D→BatchNorm →
ReLU →MaxPool →Dropout)followedbythreefullyconnected
layers, designed to capture temporal features from the pulse
signalsandmapthemtothefourgestureclasses.Duringtraining,
the network was optimized using the Adam optimizer with a
learning rate scheduler over 100 epochs and evaluated on a
held-outtestset.
